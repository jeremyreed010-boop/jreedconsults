The premise

Same grape.
Radically different wines.

Chardonnay is the great chameleon of the wine world. Plant it in chalk and it tastes like the sea. Plant it in limestone and it tastes like a struck match. Plant it in warm Napa sunshine and it tastes like a baked apple. Tonight, we taste all three, and settle the Napa family argument.

Terroir

Place is everything.

Four Chardonnays. Four climates. Four soils. The same grape finishes life as four different wines because of where it ripened and what fed its roots.

Le Mesnil-sur-Oger

49.0° N · Champagne

Pure chalk. Porous, cool, mineral. Village of saints for Blanc de Blancs. Ripens on the knife's edge.

Chablis

47.8° N · Burgundy

Kimmeridgian limestone. Ancient oyster beds, 150 million years old. Flint and brine, pulled right from the ground.

Carneros

38.2° N · Napa / Sonoma

Clay and loam over bay mud. San Pablo Bay fog rolls in nightly. Cool-climate Napa. The exception, not the rule.

Rutherford

38.4° N · Napa

Alluvial fans, "Rutherford dust." Warmer, sunnier, further from the bay. Cab country. Chardonnay plays by different rules here.

The Napa question

A tale of two Napas.

Everyone talks about "Napa Chardonnay" as if it's one thing. It isn't. The valley stretches 30 miles north from the San Pablo Bay, and that stretch is a climate gradient. Carneros, at the southern end, is cool, foggy, Pacific-touched. Rutherford, up the valley, is warmer, drier, sunnier. The same grape ripens into two completely different wines.

Wine #3 vs. Wine #4

Carneros · Rutherford

Side by side. Same grape, same vintage philosophy, 25 miles apart. One hugs the bay. The other sits mid-valley on the warm bench. The glass will show you the difference instantly.

Wine #3

Carneros

  • ClimateCool. San Pablo Bay fog rolls in nightly, holds daytime temps down.
  • SoilClay and loam over bay mud. Water-retentive, slow-ripening.
  • HarvestLater. Grapes hang longer in the cool, developing complexity.
  • WinemakingOften less new oak. Native yeast. Stainless or neutral barrel.
  • In the glassMeyer lemon, white peach, saline edge. Bright, taut acidity. Think of a California wine with a Burgundian handshake.
Wine #4

Rutherford

  • ClimateWarmer. No bay fog this far north. Long sunny afternoons.
  • SoilAlluvial fans, well-drained. The famous "Rutherford dust."
  • HarvestEarlier relative to Carneros. Sugars climb fast, acidity drops.
  • WinemakingMore new French oak common. Full malolactic. Lees stirring.
  • In the glassBaked apple, pear tart, vanilla, hazelnut, lemon curd. Round, rich, generous. Think California, undisguised.
